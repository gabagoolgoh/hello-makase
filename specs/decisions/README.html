Decision records

Short, immutable records of decisions that arrived without a spec in front of them.

When to write one

Not everything worth remembering arrives as a planned feature. A debugging fix, a config or library trade-off, a load-bearing assumption that turned out to be false — these get decided at the keyboard, with no forward spec → design → tasks chain to gate them. Those go here.

The test: was there anything to plan ahead of? An ADR records a decision as of a moment; a feature spec plans work ahead of it. Don't dress an emergent fix in the forward template — hollow "In scope / Out of scope" sections are the tell.

The rules

  • Outside the approval chain. No grill-me round, no Approved flip, no branch of its own. Write it in the session the decision was made, and move on.
  • Own numbering. NNN-slug.html starting at 001, independent of feature numbers. An ADR about feature 003 is not ADR 003.
  • Immutable. Never re-edit or re-approve one. If the decision changes later, write a new ADR superseding it and cross-link both.
  • Honest provenance. If it was decided at the keyboard and written up afterwards, say exactly that. A record that stages a deliberation nobody had is worse than no record.
  • Record the roads not taken — especially theories that were measured and falsified. A standing wrong explanation is the expensive thing: it costs the next session the same investigation. Put the numbers in.

  <p>What was happening, what was believed, what the evidence actually showed.
     A table of measurements beats adjectives.</p>
